 
Homogenisation of Cairo’s Urban Horizons 
Cultural Identity, Lived Experience, and Contemporary Urban Practice​
 
Abstract  
This dissertation investigates how the growing homogenisation of the contemporary built 
environment of Cairo impacts its own architectural identity. Through a mix of different 
methods like a literature review, analytical urban drawings, critical observation, and semi 
structured interviews with practicing architects in Egypt, this research examines how 
homogenisation rises across multiple urban scales and how architects and communities 
deal with these pressures in practice. 
The study is structured by three theoretical perspectives. First the perspective on 
globalisation and the Generic City through the lens’ of Koolhaas, Sassen and King,  followed 
by critical regionalism through Frampton, and finishing off with phenomenology of place 
with Norberg Schulz, Pallasmaa and Zumthor. Together this establishes an analytical lens 
that is used to assess Cairo’s evolving urban state. The case studies included are the New 
Administrative Capital of Cairo and different scaled districts within central Cairo to 
compare between large scale development plans, historical urban identity, and 
neighbourhoods that gradually changed through everyday life. Simultaneously revealing 
alternative approaches rooted in adaptive reuse, context specific responsiveness, and 
focused strategies. The research sheds light on ways to resist homogenisation and 
negotiate terms that reinforce Cairo’s character.
