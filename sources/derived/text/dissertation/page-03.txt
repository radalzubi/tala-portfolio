 
 
01 Introduction  
We live in an age defined by speed. Architecture today is shaped by efficiency, refinement, 
and visual simplicity with sleek façades, smooth surfaces, and quick construction methods 
that prioritise functionality and time efficiency. Buildings rise quickly, cities expand even 
quicker, and design begins to respond to convenience rather than continuity now more 
than ever. Contemporary architecture embraces clean modernism, technological 
advancement, and global unity. Yet in this pursuit of progress, something quieter has begun 
to fade. 
There was a time when architecture was crafted slowly and deliberately. There was a 
purpose. Corners were engraved, materials were layered, and façades had the work of a 
lifetime. Stone was carved through generations, details told stories, and buildings were 
made with intricate detailing, focus and patience rather than speed and profit turnover. 
Inconvenience was not avoided but embraced. The thick walls to control climate, the 
courtyards for social interactions, and the architectural detail for cultural values. 
Architecture was not just built, it was embraced and taken care of. 
Today, skylines all over Dubai, London, New York, and even Cairo, are beginning to look the 
same. Glass towers, muted palettes, and generic masterplans take over these cities, cities 
that are often seen as references of modernity and progress. As they become the 
benchmarks of finance, culture, and development, they become reference points for what 
contemporary architecture should look like, acting as a muse and model for emerging 
cities. This creates environments that feel generic no matter the place, this is 
homogenisation.  
Cairo today exists between two realities. One side, shaped by centuries of history, climate 
responsive architecture, and social life. On the other side lies New Cairo with spaces that 
are defined by large scale masterplans, gated communities, and modern developments that 
are meant to symbolize Cairo as a city of progress, modernisation, and goals. Egypt is an 
emerging global destination, especially since the opening of the Grand Egyptian Museum
