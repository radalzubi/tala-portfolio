6.4 Resisting Homogenisation through Adaptive Reuse and Context Specificity  
Yes there is a widespread concern about homogenisation across the interviews however 
the architects also claimed that there are design strategies that resist this by focusing 
solely on contextualism, adaptive reuse, and human focused spatial design. Hisham Ghorab 
spoke on projects like Emaret Kodak and Consulya, where historic exteriors are preserved 
while interiors are adapted to contemporary use: “Façades, proportions, and historic details 
must stay… interiors can adapt to modern needs.” (Ghorab) This approach shows just how 
heritage structures can be renovated without completely erasing their identity. Showing 
that preservation and modern adaptation are not incompatible and can in fact co-exist. 
Salem Mostafa also similarly described how his own practice is driven by site conditions, 
climatic response, and human behaviour. This just emphasises his careful attention to 
circulation, transitions between indoor and outdoor spaces, and spatial experience: “I try 
to design spaces that encourage movement, interaction, and a sense of belonging.” 
(Mostafa) 
All these factors and strategies align with Critical Regionalism, which prioritize the use of 
local materials, environmental integration, and site specific design as a way to fend off 
homogenisation in contemporary architecture (Frampton).
