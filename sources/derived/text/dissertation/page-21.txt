For Nehal Lehta, architectural identity is not carried in just preserved façades but is 
experienced through everyday life. She believed that West El Balad’s ongoing renovations 
should prioritise and embrace the social life and architecture rather than be like a museum 
and keep them as is in preservation: “Identity is not just heritage façades but the daily life 
they contain.” (Lehta) These inputs almost copy or mimic Norberg Schulz’s philosophy that 
true architecture is in the human interaction with space and place (Norberg Schulz), once 
again stressing that identity loss cannot be measured just by viewing the architecture but 
must truly experience the social terms of it. 
6.3 Loss of Environmental and Climate Responsiveness 
Several architects saw another aspect of homogenisation, how the detachment of 
contemporary architecture from environmental thinking and climate response was 
increasing. Salem Mostafa said the usage of sealed glass buildings disregard traditional 
simple solutions like shading, courtyards, and local material:“New projects rarely draw from 
traditional principles such as shaded circulation, passive cooling strategies, or locally 
rooted material use.” (Mostafa) 
Leithy emphasised that Cairo’s historical architecture, that integrated climate responsive 
design through form and material, has been ignored by designs that prioritise visual 
modernity over environmental logic:“Elements such as minarets, courtyards, arcades 
shared climate intelligence across eras. Today, this dialogue has fractured.” (Leithy)
