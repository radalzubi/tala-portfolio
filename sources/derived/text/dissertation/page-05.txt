 
 
 
02 Positionality  
This research is informed by my position as an Egyptian British architecture student who 
has lived between Cairo and London. Experiencing both worlds has made me understand 
questions of architectural identity, cultural unity, and globalised development. Growing up 
in Cairo allowed for firsthand experience of historic urban architecture in Zamalek and 
West el Balad as well as the Ashwa’iyat. While studying in the UK I got insight into the 
planning designs and contemporary architectural practices of the West. These experiences 
eventually influence my perception of architecture and my interest in homogenisation as 
well as becoming visually aware of its impact. 
I am aware that this background may push me to value culture over homogenisation. To 
limit this personal bias, this dissertation adopts a comparative and evidence based 
methodology, combining architectural theories, urban analysis, analytical drawings, and 
semi structured interviews with practicing architects. Rather than presenting 
homogenisation as a negative aspect, the research approaches it as a social and contextual 
process controlled by economic pressure, government decisions, and practices. 
By gaining information through semi structured interviews, researched case studies, and 
analytical drawings by urban observation, the research is balanced and subjective with 
critical analysis. This reflective approach allows architectural identity to be examined not 
as something set, but as something constantly changing by both global influence and local 
practice.
