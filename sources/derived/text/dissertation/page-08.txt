3.4 Phenomenology of Place and Lived Experience 
Critical regionalism pays attention to architectural form and environment, phenomenon 
theorists put their attention towards lived and sensory experience. Norberg Schulz says 
that architecture’s role is to create meaningful connections between people and place to 
encourage human engagements. This is believed to be done through introducing the 
concept of ‘Genius Loci', or the spirit of place (Norberg Schulz). Architecture is special by 
being able to create an experience inside a place (Norberg Schulz). 
Similarly, Pallasmaa believes in multisensory design and that architecture must have touch, 
feel, memory, and atmosphere (Pallasmaa). Zumthor shares that thought when he stressed 
the importance of material presence and emotional impact, saying that architecture must 
have meaning through sense and experience (Zumthor). 
Phenomenology changes the meaning of homogenisation, it is an experience of how spaces 
are felt, occupied, and remembered (Norberg Schulz, Pallasmaa, Zumthor). This new 
meaning of it lets architectural identity be seen in a different light, as a phenomenon that is 
constantly changing, never fixed or still. 
3.5 Egyptian Perspectives on Architecture Identity 
Egyptian architecture philosophies further elaborate on these global theories. Hassan Fathy 
speaks for climate responsive and community led architecture. While he stresses the 
importance of cultural knowledge, local materials, and socially embedded design, his work 
shows how architectural identity in Egypt historically changed (Fathy). These changes are 
because of needs, environment, and community life rather than to keep up with the global 
trends (Fathy).
