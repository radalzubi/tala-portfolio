5.4 Summary of Findings 
Across all four studies, homogenisation comes out as a complex process. It is controlled by 
economic ambition, developer control, and foreign architectural styles. The New 
Administrative Capital reflects large scale generic modernization that only prioritizes visual 
modernity over human experience. Zamalek and West El Balad reflect how historic 
neighborhoods slowly lose their character through profit driven renovation. Ashwa’iyat, 
however, reveals different ways of thinking spatially, socially and fluidly.  
These case studies demonstrate that architectural identity in Cairo is not vanishing 
overtime, but is being renovated bit by bit. While formal developments adopt globalised 
aesthetics thinking they are improving the city, informal neighbourhoods and adaptive 
historic concepts continue to defend their culturally rich environment.  
 
06 Architects Perspective on Homogenisation in Cairo 
The purpose of the interviews with the architects was to understand how practicing 
architects perceive the rise of architectural homogenisation in their city. The purpose was 
also to understand the driving forces as well as the interference it has with cultural identity 
and experience. The following analysis will integrate the recurring statements and specific 
quotes from the architects and place them within debates on globalisation, urban memory, 
and design practice discovered from previous theories and philosophies researched.
