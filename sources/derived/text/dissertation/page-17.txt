 
 
In contrast to formal developments, Ashwa’iyat, organically grown informal 
neighbourhoods, which may seem odd to analyze, but the Ashwa’iyat are a large part of 
Cairo’s history. It reveals an alternative of urban production that solely focuses on needs, 
adaptation, and community. Analytical drawings show the compact building and spatial 
organization as well as the flexibility and accessibility to add construction overtime. 
 
Figure 9, Street View Ashwa’iyat Façade  
Unlike planned developments, Ashwa’iyat change over time, responding directly to 
residents’ needs rather than masterplans from large developing practices. Developers 
always sought out Ashwa’iyat residents to take over the land to build a modern 
development however this would leave hundreds or thousands misplaced. Instead artists 
decided to make the Ashwa’iyat more memorable and create murals and façade designs on 
top of the existing structure (Molloy). They collectively express identity, culture and rooted 
memory into the urban fabric.
