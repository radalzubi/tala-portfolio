# Brief — Tala Hagras, The Living Section

Working concept: **The Living Section**. Architecture becomes understandable through
movement: a façade unfolds, a section opens, a route becomes legible, an urban drawing
shows how a city is lived in. The site is a digital exhibition of Tala Hagras's
selected architectural work (2024–2026) and her dissertation research on Cairo.

Three things a visitor must grasp quickly:
1. This is Tala Hagras's architectural work.
2. Her interests: design, material, movement, atmosphere and place.
3. The projects carry design reasoning and technical depth worth exploring.

Editorial headline (new website copy, not a source quotation): “Architecture, experienced in motion.”

### Project 1 — Music Institution / Architecture as Music (p.3–7), Reading, UK
Circular performance space anchors the project; landscaped, stepped routes connect city and canal.
p.3 design statement collage + external render; p.4 site plan (circle, curved paths, canal) + site
analysis collage (contains photographs of graffiti walls, authorship not stated); p.5 section with
construction legend CT01–CT20 and space-use legend SU01– (recording studios, double-height performance
space, stepped seating, café, mezzanine) + exploded axonometric; p.6 material reuse study referencing
reclaimed glulam from the ABBA Arena (third-party precedent photographs inside a composite), timber
connection detail drawings, 1:200 site model, 1:20 tectonic model, section model; p.7 renders
(external, internal of the circular hall) + model photos. Reclaimed-timber precedent is a design
intention, not a built fact.

### Project 2 — Maison Peplum (p.8–12), Jupps Warehouse, Brentford, London
Adaptive reuse. Peplum-jacket folds inform a parametric façade of repeated curved timber fins over
an existing pitched-roof warehouse shell; programme for making, exhibition and community use.
p.8 concept collage + exterior render (only 358 × 228 px); p.9 garment deconstruction drawings,
tectonic model response (text inside image), 1:200 parametric façade model; p.10 ground plan and roof
plan in context (canal), programme list (café, outlet and showroom, washroom stalls, principal studios,
haberdashery and textiles supplies, offices, storage) + exploded axonometric; p.11 section 1:50
(existing brick/steel-framed shell, two floors, fins on both sides, cantilever steel beam, steel
cross bracing, canal to one side) + façade assembly list (unistrut connector, hanger rib, fin support
rib, fins, screen brackets, glazing wall frame, glazing spandrels, cantilever steel beam, mullions and
transoms, cross-bracing steel frames) + wall build-up detail (existing timber/steel frames, insulation,
CLT cladding, brick cladding, hanging rib, support panel, spacer, ventilation); p.12 night render,
interior render, 1:50 section model, 1:200 façade model. Raster images are 358–687 px wide.

### Project 3 — Sound Aroma Loom (p.13–16), Whitechapel Market, London
Text only: an experimental pavilion relating sound, aroma, textile and urban experience; flower-like
chimneys, linen curtains and a rainwater strategy. No artwork supplied. Text-led secondary entry.

### Research — Cairo (portfolio p.17 + dissertation)
Portfolio p.17 uses the editorial heading “Beyond the Generic City”. The dissertation title is the
publication title. Cases: New Administrative Capital; Zamalek; West el Balad; Ashwa'iyat (informal
neighbourhoods) — different spatial conditions and scales. Figures 1–9 and 11 are drawn by Tala
Hagras (red pencil over black linework). Figure 10 is a third-party photograph (credit: Seed, El.
(2016), via The Telegraph) and is not reproduced on the site. No numerical scores, GIS or time series.
