01. THE CREATIVE THESIS

Working concept: THE LIVING SECTION.

Architecture becomes understandable through movement: a façade unfolds, a section opens, a public route becomes legible, and an urban drawing reveals how a city is lived in. Build a digital exhibition around that idea. Combine the precision of an architectural drawing, the restraint of a beautifully typeset monograph, and the spatial presence of a well-lit physical model.

The visitor should understand three things quickly:
1. This is Tala Hagras's architectural work.
2. Her interests include material, movement, atmosphere, and place.
3. The projects have design reasoning and technical depth worth exploring.

Suggested editorial headline, which is newly written website copy rather than a source quotation:
“Architecture, experienced in motion.”

Keep the actual project titles prominent. The exhibition concept can live in the interaction system and small editorial labels; it must not obscure Tala's name or become a fictional practice name.

Choose Maison Peplum as the initial hero candidate because its folded, repeated façade offers a distinctive sculptural silhouette. Validate this against the extracted artwork and the quality of the available reconstruction. Use the music institution as hero instead if its visual result is demonstrably stronger. Make one decision and carry it consistently through composition, lighting, transitions, and project sequencing.

The site must be beautiful as a still composition. Motion should reveal a relationship or give the visitor control. Every large visual effect must earn its place.

On touch devices, normal scrolling takes priority until the visitor explicitly engages the viewer. Provide an exit from model interaction and test the gesture contract. No accidental scroll traps or body-scroll locks left behind after closing a viewer.

Reduced motion means no automatic spinning, camera flights, large parallax or forced scroll-linked movement. Preserve the information using still views, explicit view switches and modest fades when suitable.

If rotation starts automatically and continues, make pausing easy and durable. A user's pause choice must not reset merely because focus or hover changes. Do not automatically resume after they have deliberately paused. Pause animation offscreen and when the tab is hidden.

Use native scrolling as the baseline. If adding scroll smoothing, justify it, support opt-out/reduced motion and test anchors, keyboard scrolling, Back navigation and drawing viewers. Omit it if it compromises the experience.

10. PERFORMANCE AND PROGRESSIVE ENHANCEMENT

Render identity, navigation, summaries and source artwork as accessible HTML. Load a properly sized poster immediately. The portfolio should remain useful with JavaScript disabled or WebGL unavailable.

Lazy-load 3D code and assets after the first meaningful content, near viewport entry or on explicit interaction. Do not hide the entire page behind a 3D loader. Only instantiate the necessary viewer; avoid a persistent canvas for every project card. Coordinate ownership if using one shared scene renderer.

Use on-demand rendering while the scene is at rest. If optional rotation or animation is active, explicitly schedule the needed frames; a demand-only renderer does not animate by itself. Stop the frame loop when paused, hidden or outside the viewport, then invalidate when controls or state change.

Adapt scene quality to observed performance. Cap device pixel ratio, reuse geometries/materials, instance repeated fins where appropriate, minimise expensive shadows and dispose of resources on route changes. Avoid React state updates on every animation frame. Keep the fallback poster available after errors or WebGL context loss.

Starting engineering budgets, to be measured and revised with evidence:
- Non-3D first-load JavaScript: aim below 200 KB gzip; record framework overhead and any justified exception.
- Typical responsive hero poster: aim at or below 250 KB where visual quality permits.
- First interactive model: aim below 4 MB compressed transfer; do not fetch all models on entry.
- Prefer 1K–2K textures unless closer inspection demonstrates a need for more.
- Start below roughly 150,000 visible triangles and 100 draw calls per active portfolio scene, then profile actual devices. These are project targets, not universal limits.
- Aim for smooth interaction around 60 FPS on a documented capable desktop and at least 30 FPS on a representative supported mobile device. If only emulation is available, say so and keep physical-device validation outstanding.

Core Web Vitals targets: LCP at or below 2.5 seconds, INP at or below 200 ms and CLS at or below 0.1, evaluated at the 75th percentile of real visits once sufficient field data exists. Before launch, report lab measurements and interaction traces honestly; do not label a Lighthouse score as proof of field INP or guaranteed real-user performance.

Document viewport, device/emulation, browser, throttling, test route, cold/warm cache conditions and asset sizes. Use fewer visuals or a poster-first mobile path when that makes the site stronger.
